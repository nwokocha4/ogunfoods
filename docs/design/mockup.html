Green = do thisEvery primary button and every completed step. One action colour, so the eye always knows where to go.
Orange = moneyFees, totals, payment warnings. Keeps cash visually separate from navigation.
Cream background, white cardsWarm rather than clinical — it reads as food, and white cards stay legible on a bright Lagos afternoon.

Type & size

System font, no downloadsLoads instantly on cheap Android phones. Headings are tight (-2% to -3% letter-spacing); body text never below 12.5px.
390px firstDesigned for a phone screen. Nothing needs a desktop layout until Phase 6.
Big tap targetsButtons are 44–52px tall. The agent page is all large buttons — usable one-handed, in the rain.

Decisions worth arguing about

1. The money breakdown never hidesSubtotal, delivery, service fee and total are always four visible lines. Hidden fees are the fastest way to lose a customer.
2. "Pay first" is stated twiceAt checkout and again on the confirmation page. Customers who skip it will call you — so the page has to answer before they do.
3. The reference number is the loudest thing on the confirmation screenBecause matching transfers manually only works if they type it.
4. Progress bar stops at DeliveredCancelled is a separate card state (screen 9), not a step — it should never look like a stage the order moved through.
5. No bottom tab bar on checkoutCheckout, confirmation and the agent page are single-purpose. Removing navigation stops people bouncing out mid-payment.
